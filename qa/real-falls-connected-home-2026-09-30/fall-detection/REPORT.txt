ONE fall detection investigation
Real video evidence and the 20 fps question
30 September 2026 • Technical research report

The current fall rule needs stronger temporal activity reasoning and a measured inference cadence. The
evidence does not establish that swapping in a pose detector, or simply sending more frames, will solve the
missed falls and false alarms.
Across 128 evaluation clips, the existing ONE path at 5 Hz detected 37 of 63 annotated falls and triggered on 5 of
65 daily-activity clips. At the idealized current 1.4-second interval, it detected 2 of 63 falls. These are offline,
cold-start clip results, not estimates of continuous deployment reliability.

What the evidence supports
Sampling matters. The browser currently submits at most one JPEG every 1.4 seconds and skips a tick while a
request is active. A camera stream at 20 fps does not mean the backend receives or processes 20 frames per
second.
Twenty Hz did not improve recall in the paired test. On the same nine evaluation clips, 5 Hz and 20 Hz each
detected 2 of 4 falls; false-positive ADL clips increased from 1 of 5 to 2 of 5. The result is a small diagnostic
comparison, not a general ranking of frame rates.
Pose remains an open research option. At 5 Hz, pose boxes detected 30 of 63 falls, versus 37 for ONE, with five
false-positive ADL clips for each. The same tracker and box rule were used; a trained temporal skeleton classifier
was not tested.
Recommended next step: compare time-based temporal logic and activity models under realistic capture-to-
alert timing, including continuous pre-fall history and long non-fall recordings. Preserve a separate evaluation
set before choosing thresholds.

Dataset and evaluation protocol
Accuracy was evaluated on public GMDCSA-24 RGB videos of consenting actors staging falls and daily activities
at home. Scenes include low light, seated and chair falls, partial views, lying down, bending, exercise and bed
activities. [1, 2]

        Partition               Subject folders        Fall       ADL         Clips         Samples at 5 Hz

        Development                    1               16          16          32                1,007

        Evaluation                   2 to 4            63          65          128               5,432

        Total                        1 to 4            79          81          160               6,439


All 160 video hashes match the pinned revision. They contain 34,172 native frames and about 21.5 minutes of
material. Both models completed all 6,439 samples with no missing or duplicate frame IDs. The manifest yields
79 Fall and 81 ADL clips, reversing the paper’s reported totals; the manifest governs scoring.

Partitioning and prior exposure
Methods were fixed before the initial evaluation. No weights were trained or fine-tuned, and no held-out
threshold tuning was performed. The expanded set includes the earlier 32-clip pilot; results beyond the pilot are
reported separately.
The split is by subject folder. The paper describes four actors in its main text but three in its ethics statement;
identities were not independently verified. Treat this as folder-separated evaluation, without a guarantee of
person independence. [1]

Replay and scoring
Each clip resets detector-associated tracking and fall state. Frames are resized to a maximum dimension of 640
pixels and encoded at JPEG quality 58, approximating the browser canvas quality of 0.58. The real low-light
preprocessing, detector, stability tracker and fall rule are replayed offline using source timestamps. This is not a
full HTTP or app deployment test.
A fall clip is positive when the rule emits an alert at or after the source fall onset minus 0.2 seconds; ADL is
positive for any alert. These are clip-level event labels, not person-localized fall ground truth. Latency is the first
qualifying alert time minus the annotation time. One fall clip, s4_fall_15, lacks onset timing: it remains in
classification counts and is omitted from latency.

Cold start changes the interpretation
Three observations need a minimum span of 2.8 seconds at the 1.4-second interval, 0.4 seconds at 5 Hz and 0.1
seconds at 20 Hz. Of 62 evaluation falls with onset timing, 41 begin before 2.8 seconds; neither detector path
caught any of these at the 1.4-second interval. These results do not estimate continuous-monitoring
performance.

but are not production-ready alternatives.

Identity diagnostic: force one ID onto the highest-confidence person prediction. Confidence diagnostic: keep detector outputs above the
0.20 detection floor, but bypass the fall rule’s 0.55 gate. Neither is a deployment recommendation.

What the ablations show
For ONE, forcing one identity recovered three additional falls; removing the confidence gate recovered four,
with unchanged false-positive clip counts. For pose, forcing one identity recovered three falls but added two
false-positive clips. Association and confidence gating contribute to misses, but most missed falls remained.
These diagnostics do not establish safety on multiple people or long non-fall recordings.
The pilot had 8 development and 24 evaluation clips. ONE detected 9 of 12 falls at 5 Hz with one false-positive
ADL clip. The expanded results provide a less favorable and more useful basis for the next experiment.

Variation within the expanded set
On the 104 evaluation clips beyond the pilot, ONE detected 28 of 51 falls and alerted on 4 of 53 ADL clips. Pose
boxes detected 22 of 51 and alerted on 3 of 53. These additional examples account for the lower full-set recall.
For clips marked night, ONE detected 5 of 11 falls and pose boxes 4 of 11. By day, they detected 32 of 52 and 26
of 52, respectively. Scene and activity also vary, so this comparison does not isolate lighting as a cause.

Metrics that should not be overread
Each base detector path has five false-positive ADL clips containing seven alerts. Counting each false alert but
only one detection per fall clip gives 84.1% precision for ONE and 81.1% for pose, versus 88.1% and 85.7% by
clip. The artificial fall prevalence means neither estimates alert quality in an occupied home. False-positive clips
do not estimate false alarms per camera-day.
Counts of frames containing a person prediction are coverage diagnostics, not person-detection recall. There are
no verified person boxes for that claim, and the pose model sometimes labels hanging clothes as another
person. Reported event latency excludes processing, network and UI delay, and only includes detected falls.

Faster sampling and runtime feasibility
Twelve clips were selected before 20 Hz inference: three development and nine evaluation. The evaluation
subset has four falls and five ADL clips, with 1,287 samples. Lower cadences use paired subsamples of the same
cached detector outputs.

Nine-clip subset only. One fall changes recall by 25 percentage points. Latency is conditional on detection and excludes processing delay.
A dash means undefined.

Keeping three hits while increasing cadence shortens the rule’s minimum evidence span. The separate nine-hit
diagnostic at 20 Hz restores a 0.4-second minimum span, but does not make every aspect of the temporal logic
duration-invariant. It detected one fall and raised one false-positive clip. Higher cadence alone did not resolve
the activity ambiguity.
All nine evaluation videos have native gaps below 50 ms. The three development videos have a few larger gaps,
where resampling may hold a frame. Twenty-nine full-set videos are about 15 fps and cannot supply 20
independent observations per second.
On the full set, shifting the 1.4-second sampling grid across seven phase offsets produced only 0 to 2 detected
falls for ONE and 0 to 3 for pose, with no false-positive ADL clips.

Serial 20 Hz inference needs roughly 50 ms per frame; this CPU path averaged 148 ms. Benchmark the complete
request path on deployment hardware before changing cadence. No Mac, GPU or end-to-end throughput result
is claimed.

Observed failure mechanisms and next experiments
Low confidence after the fall
In pilot clip s3_fall_16, the detector produced eight upright, one transition and nine low-posture boxes above its
0.20 floor. Only the upright boxes survived the fall rule’s 0.55 confidence gate. Removing that gate recovered
the fall. The next test should assess calibrated confidence and low-light temporal evidence while measuring
additional false alarms.

Seated and partial views
Clip s3_fall_13 began in a low seated posture and stayed low through the fall; lowering confidence did not
recover it. The code can fall back to the previous center when no upright reference exists, so an upright
observation is not an absolute prerequisite. Its box geometry still lacks a useful transition here. The partial chair
fall s4_fall_10 remained missed after either the identity or confidence diagnostic.

Ordinary activity that resembles a fall
The push-up clip s4_adl_05 triggered both ONE and the pose-box path at 4.4 seconds in the 5 Hz pilot replay.
Better person detection does not remove this activity-specificity problem. A falling body and deliberate exercise
can share a low box and downward movement.

Experiments to run before choosing an approach
1 Measure the live timing path. Record capture, upload, inference, tracking and alert timestamps, skipped
requests, queue age and p50/p95 latency. Compare 5, 10 and 20 Hz targets on the actual deployment hardware
using newest-frame processing rather than an accumulating queue.
2 Make evidence duration explicit. Compare time-based stabilization and fall-confirmation windows, with
continuous pre-roll, against the unchanged rule. Isolate cadence from the amount of temporal evidence instead
of reusing an unqualified frame count.
3 Compare temporal representations. Evaluate RGB motion, tracked box trajectories and a trained temporal
skeleton classifier on the same partitions. Include seated falls, occlusion, low light, bed transitions and exercise.
Pose should earn its role through this comparison.
4 Use a fresh external evaluation. Preserve untouched people and environments before tuning. Add long ADL
streams and report false alarms per camera-hour, missed falls, onset latency and performance by scenario. Use
repeated runs or multiple seeds for trained models.
None of these findings establishes medical or emergency-response reliability. The investigation is evidence for
the next engineering experiment, not a production fix or a safety validation.

height; three low confirmations within four seconds.

Limits that affect decisions
Short staged clips, coarse onset annotations, uncertain actor mapping, overlapping pilot exposure, one missing
onset and no verified person-level fall annotations constrain the conclusions. One CSV lists s2_adl_04 as 70
seconds; its decoded duration is about 7.84 seconds. Native timestamps and decoded durations are used where
metadata conflicts. This investigation makes no production-code changes, merge or deployment.
